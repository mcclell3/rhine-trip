A McClelland vacation · for three

Amsterdam & the Rhine Valley

Friday 9 – Saturday 17 October 2026

Canals at dusk. A grand hotel across from Cologne Cathedral. A river sliding past the Loreley. Rheingau wine at the table. Nine days, written for you.

Day 1

Amsterdam landing

Friday 9 October 2026

Hannah and Mom land at Schiphol at 9:30 on United UA909. Plan about an hour for immigration, bags, and the walk to the taxi rank. The taxi to Die Port van Cleve is about 27 minutes. You asked for a 10:30 early check-in; the door is more like 11:00. Standard check-in is 15:00, so bags can wait at the desk if the rooms are not ready. Brett meets you at noon; he has been in Amsterdam since Monday.

At two you taxi to In de Waag on Nieuwmarkt. The building was a city gate and weigh house, finished in 1488. You take lunch on the main floor, now a restaurant inside those old walls.

After the meal, a taxi to the Flagship jetty at Prinsengracht 267, next to the Anne Frank House. You board at 4:00 for a one-hour cheese-and-drinks salon cruise. You do not go inside the house today; that visit is Saturday at 4:30. Be on the quay about ten minutes early.

Evening is a taxi to V&A Bar Bistro on the Prinsengracht, then back to Dam Square.

Hotel Die Port van CleveNieuwezijds Voorburgwal 176–180 · behind Dam Square, elevator.

Friday Amsterdam

Day 2

Amsterdam

Saturday 10 October 2026

Saturday opens on the Oosterdok. A morning taxi to Het Scheepvaartmuseum, the Dutch National Maritime Museum, housed in the 1656 Admiralty arsenal. Outside on the dock sits a replica of the Amsterdam, a VOC ship. Then lunch across the square at Bar Guzzo.

You come back to the hotel afterward and rest until about 4:10. The Anne Frank House is already booked: three adults, entry between 16:30 and 16:45, order N2635076021.

A taxi takes you to Prinsengracht 263, the canal house whose secret annex hid the Frank family from 1942 to 1944. About an hour inside.

Dinner is at Café de Reiger, a Jordaan brown café around the corner from the house. A neighborhood room, not a reservation circus. Taxi back to Die Port van Cleve.

Hotel Die Port van CleveNieuwezijds Voorburgwal 176–180 · second night. Elevator, behind Dam Square.

Saturday Amsterdam

Day 3

The cathedral square

Sunday 11 October 2026

A taxi around 9:20 takes you, with luggage, from Die Port van Cleve to Amsterdam Centraal. ICE 123 leaves at 10:31 from platform 4 and arrives Köln Hauptbahnhof at 13:19 on platform 6, 2 hours 48 minutes, no changes. Tickets are booked; seats are not, so add three reservations on DB Navigator. From the station it is a four-minute walk to the Excelsior Hotel Ernst at Trankgasse 1-5, a grand hotel of 1863 that faces the Dom.

Lunch at Funkhaus on Wallrafplatz, a short walk on the square. Afterward you go into Cologne Cathedral, the Gothic church begun in 1248 and finished in 1880. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site. The twin spires rise about 515 feet. Tourist interior is ticketed; the shop window on 11 Oct is 14:00-16:30.

Then EL-DE Haus on Appellhofplatz, about eight minutes from the hotel. Cologne's former Gestapo headquarters from 1935 to 1945, now the NS Documentation Center. The name is the initials of the builder, Leopold Dahmen. Open Sunday 11:00-18:00, closed Monday. Foyer tickets only. English audio guide $2. Back for dinner at Früh am Dom on the same plaza.

One night in Cologne, the river still ahead.

Excelsior Hotel ErnstTrankgasse 1–5 · across the cathedral square from the station.

Cologne

Day 4

The river drive

Monday 12 October 2026

EL-DE was Sunday. Monday morning is easy: coffee at Café Riese on Schildergasse, then a taxi to Avis at Köln Poll (Max Glomsda Str. 4). The Hauptbahnhof Avis desk is closed. Pickup at 12:00, a Seat Ateca or similar, automatic. Before you leave Cologne, phone the Altdeutsche Weinstube (+49 6722 94230) with your arrival time. The desk is staffed 7:30 AM to 6:00 PM and check-in closes at 6:00, so they need to hear from you.

From Poll you take the B9 south, the Rhine highway through the gorge, not the Autobahn. The river is the point of the day. Boppard is a Roman and medieval river town on a hairpin bend of the Rhine. Lunch is a sit-down at Le Jardin on the Bellevue terrace, about 1 hour 29 minutes from Poll. The Bellevue is the lunch stop only, not a hotel. After the meal you stay on the flat Rheinallee promenade, twenty minutes at the electoral castle, then get back in the car.

Burg Rheinfels is a thirteenth-century fortress above St. Goar, the largest castle ruin on the Rhine. Park at the walls if you want the view from the car park; do not climb the ruin. Mom’s knees do not belong on that hillside. From the Kaub bank, Pfalzgrafenstein sits in the stream, a fourteenth-century toll castle on an island, a stone ship in the current. Fifteen minutes at the lookout, no boat to the island.

The B9 is the left, west, bank and ends around Bingen. Rüdesheim is on the right bank, directly across the water. You roll onto the Bingen-Rüdesheimer car ferry at Bingen-Kempten, about 24 minutes from the Pfalz lookout. The operator runs continuously in October from 5:30 AM, last departure from Bingen 11:50 PM; at this hour a boat is typically every 15 to 20 minutes and the crossing itself is a few minutes. A car up to 3 tons is $7 (driver included), plus $2 per extra passenger, cash only on the car ferry, plus an energy surcharge the operator has levied since 21 April 2026 (they review it monthly; the current amount is on their site). From the Rüdesheim ramp it is three minutes to Grabenstraße 4. The inland detour across at Mainz or Wiesbaden adds roughly half an hour and is the fallback only if the ferry is down. On this timing you leave the Pfalz at 4:20, take the 4:45 boat, and check in around 5:00, an hour before the desk closes.

The Altdeutsche Weinstube is a sixth-generation family hotel, one street behind the Drosselgasse, with two elevators. The hotel kitchen is closed to the public, so dinner is a four-minute drive up to Eibingen. Gasthof Krancher has been in the same family since 1886, own wines, a room locals actually use. If you would rather stay in the village, Stadt Frankfurt on Marktstraße is about six minutes on cobbles, or Villa Weil on flatter Rheinstraße, though Villa Weil’s kitchen winds down toward 20:00. First of four nights, 12 to 16 October.

Altdeutsche WeinstubeGrabenstraße 4 · historic Rüdesheim, one street behind the Drosselgasse. Check-in; four nights. Two elevators. Desk closes at 6:00 PM.

The drive

Day 5

The Gorge

Tuesday 13 October 2026

You are already in a KD pier town. The Anleger is a five-minute walk downhill from Grabenstraße 4, about a fifth of a mile, mixed cobble and asphalt and not a hill climb. If the cobbles feel like a bad idea that morning, the car does it in a minute. KD’s Loreley round trip still runs on Tuesday 13 October: the 11:00 sailing is the Tuesday-to-Sunday departure, 4 April to 18 October 2026. Rüdesheim 11:00, Bingen 11:15, Assmannshausen 11:30, St. Goar 13:15, and back to the same pier at 16:20. You sit for the sailing. Lunch is on the boat; tickets bought on board are cashless.

The river slides past the Loreley, a 430-foot slate rock made famous by Heine's siren legend. Burg Katz stands above St. Goarshausen. Burg Maus is nearby on the slopes. This is the seated panoramic day Mom actually wants, and it no longer costs a drive to get to the gangway.

The same pier puts you back around 16:20. You walk, or roll, up to the hotel and rest. The gorge is already done from a chair.

Dinner is at the Ratsstube on Marktstraße, five minutes on the old-town lanes, a neighborhood room rather than the Drosselgasse show. Closed Mondays; Tuesday it is open, 12:00 to 21:00. Second night in Rüdesheim.

Altdeutsche WeinstubeGrabenstraße 4 · historic Rüdesheim. Second night. Two elevators; desk 7:30 AM–6:00 PM.

The gorge

Day 6

Gutenberg in Mainz

Wednesday 14 October 2026

Mainz is a day trip now, and Gutenberg is the reason it stays on the itinerary. You drive from Grabenstraße 4, about 35 minutes and 22 miles along the right bank, and park in one of the city garages the museum itself names: Römerpassage, Bleiche, Rheinufer, Am Brand, or Rathaus-Rheingoldhalle. Römerpassage is the short, flat walk to the door. Do not take the train. Deutsche Bahn is closing the right-bank Rhine line between Troisdorf and Wiesbaden from 10 July to 12 December 2026; every RB10 is cancelled and replaced by buses. With the car already on the trip, driving is the kinder option for Mom.

The Gutenberg Museum is not in its old house on Liebfrauenplatz. That building closed in October 2024 for a full rebuild. Since 23 November 2024 the collection has been the interim exhibition Gutenberg-Museum MOVED, inside the Natural History Museum at Reichklarastraße 1, the restored rooms of the former Clarissan convent. Wednesday hours are 9:00 to 18:00. Adult admission is $12 at the desk, a joint ticket with the Natural History Museum; there is no general-admission e-shop. Two Gutenberg Bibles are here, and the story of movable type from the 1450s. The building is certified accessible: step-free entry, an elevator, an accessible WC. The only noted obstacle is a 2-inch threshold at the door of the Bible room.

Coffee at Dom-Café on the Markt, eight minutes from the museum. Then Mainz Cathedral, the Romanesque church in red sandstone, consecrated in 1009, with six towers, two minutes from the café. Then the Sanctuary of Isis under the Römerpassage: Roman temple remains dedicated to Isis and Magna Mater, found in 1999 during mall construction. A taxi takes you to Altstadtcafé Willems for lunch. After the meal you drive back to Rüdesheim, about 36 minutes, and rest at the hotel.

Dinner is a four-minute drive to Eibingen. Eibinger Zehnthof is a family winery and gutsausschank in a 1506 stone house, own Riesling, regional plates, a room that is not the Drosselgasse. Their 2026 notice has the cellar open Wednesday to Saturday from 17:00 from 26 August; Wednesday 14 October is a serving night. Third night in Rüdesheim.

Altdeutsche WeinstubeGrabenstraße 4 · historic Rüdesheim. Third night.

Mainz

Day 7

The Rheingau

Thursday 15 October 2026

From Rüdesheim the Rheingau is next door. You drive about 32 minutes, 14 miles, through Eltville to Kloster Eberbach, a Cistercian abbey founded in 1136 beside the Steinberg vineyard. You spend the morning in the abbey: stone, barrels, the quiet of a working monastery. The interiors were used in the film The Name of the Rose. This is a car-to-door morning, which is why it fits Mom better than anything that starts on the Drosselgasse.

Lunch is at Klosterschänke, on the grounds, among the vines. Then back to the hotel to rest, about 18 minutes.

The afternoon, if you want it, is Eltville: the electoral castle and a flat Rhine promenade, 18 minutes from the hotel, roses and water rather than cobbles. The Seilbahn up to the Niederwalddenkmal is the postcard, and it is the wrong tool for this party. The valley station sits on Oberstraße, uphill through cobbles, with two steps onto the forecourt before the lift; the cabins take a wheelchair or walker only up to 33 inches and refuse electric chairs; the top is short but not a guarantee of level ground. If the Germania view still matters, drive to the visitor-centre car park at the monument and skip the cable car. Siegfried’s Mechanisches Musikkabinett is a 45-minute guided tour inside the fifteenth-century Brömserhof above the Drosselgasse, which means old floors and stairs, so it is a no for Mom.

Dinner is a five-minute drive to Weingut Magdalenenhof on Marienthaler Straße, a family gutsausschank among the vines, own Riesling, open Wednesday to Saturday from 17:00 to 23:00. Fourth night in Rüdesheim. Nothing required of the evening.

Altdeutsche WeinstubeGrabenstraße 4 · historic Rüdesheim. Fourth night. Nothing required of the evening.

The Rheingau

Day 8

A night on the square

Friday 16 October 2026

You check out of the Altdeutsche Weinstube at 11:00 and drive to Frankfurt, about 52 minutes and 40 miles. Check in at Motel One Frankfurt-Römer, Berliner Straße 55, around the corner from the square. Elevator. Breakfast is extra.

Then you linger. The Römer has been Frankfurt's city hall since 1405, stepped gables and the rooms where Holy Roman emperors were elected and feasted. Opposite stands the Ostzeile, the reconstructed half-timbered row on Römerberg. Kaiserdom St. Bartholomew is the coronation church of the Holy Roman Empire. Behind the square, the Neue Altstadt is the Dom-Römer reconstruction, opened in 2018.

Lunch is at Kleinmarkthalle on Hasengasse, the indoor market since 1954, standing or perched, whatever is open at the counters. Coffee afterward at Hoppenworth & Ploch. A taxi takes you to Main Tower, about 650 feet high, for the indoor deck on the 55th floor. The city laid out without a terrace wind.

Dinner is at Wirtshaus am Hühnermarkt, a few minutes from the hotel, in the new old town. One night on the square. The airport is a short drive in the morning.

Motel One Frankfurt-RömerBerliner Straße 55 · around the corner from Römerberg. Elevator. Breakfast extra.

Friday in Frankfurt

Day 9

Home

Saturday 17 October 2026

Breakfast if the hotel is serving that early, then you leave around 7:15 for Frankfurt Airport Terminal 1, about fifteen minutes from the Römer.

The Avis desk is Terminal 1, Area A, Level 0. You return the car there, then check in at the Lufthansa counter.

Lufthansa UA8836 leaves at 10:50, nonstop, and puts you in Chicago O'Hare at 12:55. All three of you are on this flight.
